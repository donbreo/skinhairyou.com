SKINCARE NOTES

The truth about facial serums

By the Skin Hair & You clinic team

Faster and lighter are qualities we look for in many things, including skincare. Serums are light, fast-absorbing liquids used as an alternative or in addition to creams and lotions.

What makes a serum different?

The biggest difference between a serum and a cream or lotion is often what the formulation leaves out. Many serums contain fewer occlusive moisturising ingredients, like petrolatum or mineral oil, and fewer thickening agents. Many are water-based, although formulations vary.

Serums may contain concentrated active ingredients, including antioxidants, peptides and skin-brightening ingredients such as kojic acid and arbutin. The right choice depends on your skin and the specific formulation. A serum is not a necessary step for everyone.

Serum costs, serum benefits

Concentrated active ingredients can make serums one of the more expensive products in a skincare line. Follow the product directions: a small amount is often enough, so a container may last several months.

Lightweight formulations can layer comfortably under moisturiser. Creams help lock moisture into the skin, while serums can target particular concerns. Absorption and effectiveness depend on the ingredients and formulation, not just the texture. Ask your dermatologist what your skin actually needs.

SKIN HEALTH

Understanding your skin

By the Skin Hair & You clinic team

Skin connects us to the outside world. It is part of every smile and every handshake, and it protects us from the potentially damaging influences of daily life.

Your skin is your body’s protective covering, but it has many other jobs. It acts as a water-resistant, insulating shield, helping to protect the body against changes in temperature and the environment.

The skin contributes to defence against infection and helps manufacture vitamin D, which supports healthy bones. It is also a huge sensor, packed with nerves that keep the brain in touch with the outside world.

Serving such important roles, your skin deserves to be protected, nourished and treated with care. If you notice persistent changes, a professional assessment can help you understand what is happening.

HAIR & SCALP

Understanding the types of hair loss

By the Skin Hair & You clinic team

Androgenetic alopecia

Often called male or female pattern hair loss, this is a common form of thinning associated with genetics and hormone sensitivity. In men, a receding hairline may form an M shape. In women, thinning is often more diffuse. Hormonal conditions such as PCOS can contribute in some cases, but are not the only cause.

Alopecia areata

In this autoimmune condition, the immune system attacks hair follicles, often producing circular patches of hair loss on the scalp. Genetics can play a role and more than one family member may be affected.

Traction alopecia and other causes

Tight hairstyles worn over a long period can cause traction alopecia, sometimes called chignon alopecia. Trichotillomania is a condition involving recurrent hair pulling that can cause noticeable loss. Hypotrichosis refers to sparse or absent hair growth, sometimes present from birth. These are distinct conditions and need different approaches.

Anagen and telogen effluvium

Anagen effluvium can cause sudden loss during the growing phase, often after exposure to certain medicines, chemicals or toxins. Regrowth depends on the cause. Telogen effluvium occurs when more hair follicles enter the resting phase, sometimes after physical stress, illness, surgery or other triggers.

Cicatricial (scarring) alopecia

This group of conditions destroys hair follicles and replaces them with scar tissue, which can lead to permanent loss. Early assessment is important.

Start with an assessment

If you are experiencing hair loss, you do not need to work out the cause alone. Contact our clinic to discuss an assessment and appropriate next steps.
